How a bill becomes a saving

Door 1–4: how bills come in

  • Gmail connect (when Google approves)
  • Forward a bill email
  • Upload or paste bank / card statement
  • Type it in

Statements catch the hidden ones

  • UPI AutoPay mandates
  • NACH / ECS loan EMIs
  • Card standing instructions
  • Bill date, amount, frequency: auto-detected

Confident assumptions

  • Missing a number? Due uses a typical value
  • Shown as "We assumed 8 rides a month · Change"
  • Lower confidence = ranked lower

Unknown bill? Research queue

  • App says "We're studying this bill"
  • Only the provider name is sent, never amounts or identity
  • We research, verify, and the move appears later

"People saved by switching"

  • Real, anonymous, opt-in reports only
  • Shows after 20 reports per item
  • Empty at launch, never faked

Ads (later)

  • Off at launch
  • Only when it saves that user money
  • Rules already written in Value Graph v2

Devil's advocate: the attacks and our answers

Attack
Our answer
"Nobody gives Gmail to a new app."
Statement upload and forwarding work without Gmail. Gmail is a bonus, not the core.
"Assumptions are guesses."
Labelled, editable, and ranked lower. Real receipts replace them automatically.
"Prices go stale."
Every price has a source and date; unchecked ones say "Reported price, check before switching".
"You can't cover every provider."
Unknown bills go to the research queue; the user sees "studying", not nothing.
"Free beta = no revenue."
Beta proves the saving first. Paid Plus switches on with Razorpay keys, no rebuild.
"CRED copies you."
CRED earns from loans. Due earns only from users. That can't be copied.
"Ads will kill trust."
Off at launch; later only savings-ranked, labelled, capped 3 a week.
"Social proof is empty on day 1."
Hidden until 20 real reports. Fake numbers would be a legal and trust disaster.
"Commute savings need habit change."
Marked "habit" effort, ranked below one-tap moves like plan switches.
"Lawyer risk."
No guarantee wording, no minimum amounts, information-only on loans.
