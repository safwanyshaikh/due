## 4. Launch blog post (about 700 words)

**Title: Same life. Smaller bills.**

*Meta description:* Due reads your bank and card statements on your phone and finds what you pay again and again. Free public beta in Mumbai MMR.

Look at your last bank statement. Not the balance, the lines. Somewhere between rent, groceries and the chai stall there is a different kind of line: the one that comes back every month. A phone recharge. An OTT plan. A card payment that includes interest. An EMI. A ride pass. A cloud storage plan you started for one trip.

Most of us know these exist. Few of us can list them without opening the statement. That is the gap Due was built for.

**What Due is**

Due by KAI is an app for India that reads your bank, card and loan statements on your phone, finds the recurring payments, and shows ranked "moves" that could cost you less. The statement is read on your device. Due is information only: it does not pay, cancel, pause, switch or lend. It shows what it found and where the information comes from. You decide.

**Why on the phone**

Statements are personal. We built Due so the reading and the maths happen on your phone. The server only serves public rules, such as published plan prices and offer terms, and receives anonymous, number-free requests when you opt in. We do not read your SMS and we do not sell your data to lenders.

**What a "move" looks like**

A move is a plain suggestion with its source. For example (Illustrative, not a promise):

- "Your phone plan renews on the 12th. A different published plan has more data for a similar price. Check the provider page before switching."
- "Your card statement shows a finance charge. Paying the full due amount by the due date avoids it."
- "Your EMI is on a floating-rate loan. RBI rules have barred prepayment charges on such loans to individuals since 1 January 2026, so ask the lender what the exit cost is." (Check the RBI notification and your lender's letter before acting.)

Where Due does not know a price, it says so. It never invents numbers.

**Who it is for**

If you are a salaried commuter in MMR, with a train or bus pass, a phone plan, a card or two, and a streaming plan you rarely open, Due gives you the list in minutes. If you run a small business or a shop, Due's business profile keeps those spends separate. If you manage the household bills, it shows what is due this month and what repeats.

**Why we are not like the others**

Many finance apps earn from lenders or merchants. Due earns only from its users, and today it earns nothing, because the public beta is free. We make no lending offers. A later Plus tier is planned, with a price hypothesis of Rs 149 a month or Rs 1,299 a year, set by us and open to your feedback. Nothing is charged during the beta.

**What we promise, and what we do not**

We promise to be clear about what Due does and does not do. We do not promise that you will save any amount. Some people will find a lot, some very little. Due shows what it found, labelled as found, and shows what you saved only when your own receipts say so.

**How to try it**

1. Get your latest bank or card statement from your bank's app or net banking.
2. Open it in Due. The reading happens on your phone.
3. See your recurring list, your ranked moves, and the official source for each.
4. Tell us what is wrong. The beta exists to find that out.

**What's next**

We are starting in Mumbai MMR, including Navi Mumbai and Thane, because that is where we live and commute. We will add more providers, more categories and more cities as the research queue fills, based on what you ask for.

If you try it, we would love to hear what Due found, what it missed, and what felt unclear. Your feedback shapes the next version.
