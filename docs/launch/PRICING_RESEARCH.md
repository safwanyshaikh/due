## 4. Willingness-to-pay logic

1. **Anchor to what the product finds, not to peers.** Due's internal measurement design (research page, not advertised) ties the value to Rs found versus Plus fee. This is an internal KPI only, never a promise.
2. **Indian anchors.** A person paying Rs 149 for Netflix Mobile or Rs 499 a year for JioHotstar Mobile can see Rs 149 a month as "one more subscription." The benchmark that matters is whether Due finds more than it costs for a typical user.
3. **Savings-to-price ratio (arithmetic, not a claim).**

| Plus price | Annual fee | Rs found needed per month to equal the fee | To equal 2x the fee |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rs 99 per month | Rs 1,188 | Rs 99 | Rs 198 |
| Rs 149 per month | Rs 1,788 | Rs 149 | Rs 298 |
| Rs 1,299 per year | Rs 1,299 | Rs 108 | Rs 217 |
| Rs 999 per year | Rs 999 | Rs 83 | Rs 167 |

   Use the beta's real found-Rs distribution (aggregated, n >= 20) to decide whether Rs 1,299 is reachable for the median user. If the median found is far below Rs 108 a month, Plus must justify itself through alerts, Gmail scan, sync and watching, not through savings alone.
4. **Paying segment matters.** Salaried commuters (P1) have lower willingness to pay; proprietors (P2) will pay if time saved or avoidable costs are visible.
5. **Free beta hazard.** A free beta teaches users the product is free. Mitigation: label "Plus, free during beta", and show the future price in the survey.

## 5. Recommended tiers and launch price

These are hypotheses to be tested with the survey below, in line with `PRICING.md` ("hypotheses, not final prices"; admin-set).

| | Free | Plus | Business |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Price at launch of paid flow | Rs 0 | **Rs 149 per month or Rs 1,299 per year** (keeps existing hypothesis; yearly = about 27% below 12 x monthly) | Hypothesis: **Rs 299 per month or Rs 2,999 per year**, to validate (about 4x Vyapar mobile annual of Rs 699 to 799; within the Rs 3,399 to 4,800 desktop band) |
| Who | Anyone tracking their own bills | People who want Due to find and watch for them | Proprietors and small teams (not built yet) |
| Core | Add items, statement import on device, 7 and 30 day view, in-app reminders, CSV export, limited uploads (5 a month per `PRICING.md`) | Gmail read-only scan, forwarding, unlimited uploads, email reminders, offer matches, sync and backup, loan watch, expiry alerts | Business profile, shared access, vendor and card-interest review, accountant export |
| Beta treatment | Live | Free during public beta | Not sold |

Reasoning:
- Keep Free genuinely useful (statement import and first moves) so activation and aha happen before any paywall; this is the value proof that drives paid conversion.
- Plus at Rs 149 / Rs 1,299 sits above Indian OTT mobile tiers and well below global finance apps (USD 6 to 15 a month). The annual price is a clear nudge toward commitment with no surprise renewals (per `PRICING.md`).
- Do NOT charge until: billing terms and legal review are done, at least 300 activated users, W4 retention hypothesis met, and the survey supports a price band.
- **Founding-member option** (test, not decided): beta users who opt in may lock Rs 999 a year for year one. Limit to the first 500, disclose that it is a limited offer, no refund guarantee language.
- Household/Family plan (Rs 249 per month, Rs 2,199 per year hypothesis, already in code but off) stays off until sharing exists. Do not combine with Business.
- Refund policy: follow platform law and Razorpay terms; do not advertise a savings-linked refund (the 2x refund is an internal design; the lawyer note says do not advertise a guarantee).

Alternative if survey shows low willingness to pay: Plus at Rs 99 per month or Rs 799 per year (the value-graph's illustrative Rs 99 assumption). Decision rule below.

## 6. Three-question pricing survey for beta users

Show only to activated users after their first aha, in app, 1 minute, skippable. Record answers without statement data.

1. **Value gap (Van Westendorp style, two parts).** "At what monthly price would Due Plus feel (a) a bargain, (b) getting too expensive to consider?" Free-entry Rs field for each.
2. **Choice test.** "If Plus (Gmail scan, alerts, sync, loan watch) cost Rs 149 a month or Rs 1,299 a year, what would you do? Options: Pay yearly / Pay monthly / Use Free only / Would need it cheaper / Not sure."
3. **What would make it worth it.** "What is the main reason you would pay (or not pay)? Options: Alerts before renewals / Found Rs more than the fee / Auto scan of email / Sync across devices / For my business / I would not pay / Other."

Decision rule (proposed): if at least 20% pick a paid option at Rs 149 / Rs 1,299 and the median "getting too expensive" answer is above Rs 149, keep the hypothesis. If under 10% pick paid, test Rs 99 / Rs 799 and re-run. Treat n < 100 as directional only.
